# Newton's First Law

An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion at constant velocity, unless acted on by a net external force. This is the law of inertia. Mass is a measure of inertia. A frame where the first law holds is called an inertial frame.

# Newton's Second Law

The net external force on an object equals its mass times acceleration:

$$\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = m\vec{a}.$$

This is a vector equation: apply it component by component. The net force is the vector sum of all forces acting on the object. The unit of force is the newton: $1\ \text{N} = 1\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}^2$.

# Newton's Third Law

For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. If object A exerts a force on B, then B exerts a force on A of the same magnitude but opposite direction. The two forces act on different objects, so they never cancel on a single object. When drawing a free-body diagram, include only forces acting ON the object, not forces the object exerts on others.

# Free-Body Diagrams

A free-body diagram shows an object as a point and all external forces as arrows. Draw one per object of interest. Common forces: weight $W = mg$ (downward), normal force $N$ (perpendicular to surface), tension $T$ (along a rope), friction $f$ (along a surface, opposing motion or tendency), and applied pushes/pulls.

# Friction

Kinetic friction acts on an object sliding over a surface:

$$f_k = \mu_k N,$$

where $\mu_k$ is the coefficient of kinetic friction and $N$ the normal force. Static friction adjusts to prevent motion up to a maximum:

$$f_s \le \mu_s N.$$

Friction always opposes the relative motion or the impending motion. On a level surface with no other vertical forces, $N = mg$; on an incline, $N = mg\cos\theta$.
