# Simple Harmonic Motion

Simple harmonic motion (SHM) is oscillation in which the restoring force is proportional to the displacement from equilibrium:

$$F = -kx.$$

For a mass on a spring the period is

$$T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}},$$

and for a simple pendulum (small angle) the period is

$$T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}.$$

The period depends on the system's properties, not on amplitude.

# Frequency and Period

The period $T$ is the time for one full cycle; the frequency $f$ is cycles per second. They are reciprocals:

$$f = \frac{1}{T}.$$

The SI unit of frequency is the hertz (Hz); $1\ \text{Hz} = 1\ \text{s}^{-1}$.

# Wave Properties

A wave is a disturbance that transfers energy without transferring matter. For a periodic wave, the wavelength $\lambda$ is the distance between successive crests, and the wave speed relates to frequency and wavelength by

$$v = f\lambda.$$

This holds for all waves (sound, light, water). For a wave on a string, $v = \sqrt{F_T/\mu}$, where $F_T$ is the tension and $\mu$ the linear mass density.

# Transverse and Longitudinal Waves

In a transverse wave, the disturbance is perpendicular to the direction of propagation (waves on a string). In a longitudinal wave, the disturbance is parallel to the propagation (sound in air). Both obey $v = f\lambda$.
