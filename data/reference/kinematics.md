# Displacement and Velocity

Displacement is the change in position of an object, a vector with magnitude and direction. Average velocity is the displacement divided by the elapsed time:

$$\bar{v} = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}.$$

Instantaneous velocity is the limit as $\Delta t \to 0$. Speed is the magnitude of velocity, a scalar. The SI unit is m/s.

# Acceleration

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity:

$$\bar{a} = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}.$$

It is a vector. Constant (uniform) acceleration means $\bar{a} = a$ at all times. The SI unit is m/s$^2$. A negative acceleration means the acceleration points opposite to the chosen positive direction — it does not necessarily mean the object is slowing down (slowing depends on the relative directions of $v$ and $a$).

# Kinematic Equations for Constant Acceleration

When acceleration is constant, four equations relate displacement, velocity, acceleration, and time:

$$v = v_0 + at$$
$$x = x_0 + v_0 t + \tfrac12 at^2$$
$$v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a(x - x_0)$$
$$\bar{v} = \tfrac12(v_0 + v)$$

Choose the equation that contains the one unknown you are solving for and the knowns you have. These apply only when $a$ is constant; if acceleration varies, use the work-energy theorem or energy methods instead.

# Free Fall

Free fall is motion under gravity alone, with constant downward acceleration $g \approx 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$ near Earth's surface. Air resistance is neglected. The kinematic equations apply with $a = -g$ (taking up as positive). An object thrown upward slows at $g$, momentarily stops at the peak, then speeds up symmetrically on the way down.
